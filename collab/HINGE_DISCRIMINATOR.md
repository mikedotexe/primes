# Hinge Discriminator

**Updated**: April 2026
**Purpose**: freeze the exact target and evidence boundary of the hinge
discriminator pass

This note sits beneath the broader cross-base hinge/species language in
[`HARDENED_RESEARCH_PROGRAMS.md`](./HARDENED_RESEARCH_PROGRAMS.md). The
follow-up note [`HINGE_ATOM_FAMILIES.md`](./HINGE_ATOM_FAMILIES.md) classifies
the deterministic atom families by depth and theorem proximity once the target
and shortcut boundary are fixed.

## Exact Target

The target of the discriminator search is the current `persistent_core`
phenomenon in the maintained `2p` hinge lane:

- persistence from `M=1` into `M=2`
- shared-overlap dominance at `M=2`

The search is not trying to prove a universal base law. It is trying to answer
the narrower explanatory question:

> What exact finite feature, or smallest exact rule family, best separates the
> base-`14` hinge witnesses from the nearby split species?

## Banned Shortcuts

The primary search intentionally forbids tautological restatements of the
target. In particular, it does not allow:

- `hinge_category`
- `shared_yield_core`
- `m1_to_m2_persistent`
- string labels that are just renamed target classes

Those fields remain useful for interpretation and auditing, but not as search
atoms in the main discriminator pass.

## Exact Rules vs Species Labels

Two layers must stay separate:

- **Exact rule layer**:
  finite count/sign/threshold predicates over the bounded-`k` feature surface
- **Species label layer**:
  report-language buckets such as `persistent_core`, `persistence_only`,
  `core_only`, and `active_neither`

The species labels summarize the current ecology. They are not themselves the
discriminator.

## Current Boundary

This pass hardens the hinge explanation. It does **not** establish:

- a universal theorem about all `2p` bases
- a general `M>=3` proof
- a density theorem
- a chaos/dynamical result

The maintained boundary is:

- Lean/Agda certify exact transfer and compactness substrate where available
- Rust reports search for exact or near-exact finite discriminators on the
  maintained catalog
- cross-base species language stays empirical unless translated into exact
  finite conditions

## Practical Reading

If the report finds an exact separator, that upgrades the explanatory language.
If it finds `no_exact_rule`, that is still positive signal when the remaining
frontier is small, interpretable, and stable across reruns. The important thing
is to avoid pretending that a near-frontier rule is already a theorem-shaped
discriminator.
