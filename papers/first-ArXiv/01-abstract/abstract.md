# Abstract

We present a novel approach to prime number generation through symmetric polynomial constructions called "membrane polynomials." By discovering that these polynomials create linear patterns in residue space modulo small primes, we transform the computationally intensive primality testing problem into a GPU-friendly affine computation. Our method achieves 25-30% prime density compared to the 4.5% expected from random 32-bit integers, while enabling parallel evaluation at 186 million candidates per second on consumer GPUs. The key innovation is the pre-computation of affine signatures (s,g) for each prime p, reducing divisibility tests from expensive modular arithmetic to simple multiply-add operations. We provide both theoretical analysis of why membrane structures favor primality and empirical validation across multiple number bases. The implementation achieves a 1000x speedup over traditional CPU-based approaches through hardware-algorithm co-design. This work suggests a broader principle: many seemingly complex computational problems may hide linear structure when viewed in the appropriate coordinate system.
