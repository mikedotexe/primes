# Agent Collaboration Charter

This is a reusable collaboration pattern for developing new ideas without
letting enthusiasm outrun evidence.

## Core Principle

Treat the work as a promising program that needs both protection and pressure.

- protect the signal
- pressure the claims

If the idea is real, this process strengthens it. If part of it is weak, the
same process reveals the narrower version that actually matters.

## Three-Layer View

Most early idea-heavy repositories contain three layers:

- the underlying idea or construction
- the measured or formal support
- the interpretation, metaphor, or narrative wrapper

Progress comes from separating these layers and improving each one on purpose.

## Operating Rules

- preserve novelty where support exists
- mark `verified`, `empirical`, and `open` separately
- steelman before dismissing
- prefer exact language to exciting language
- keep null results and counterexamples visible
- archive stale framing instead of letting it distort the active surface
- treat sparks of insight as assets: log them, refine them, and convert them
  into concrete next probes

## Working Method

For each promising thread, maintain three notes:

### Verified

What is already supported by code, tests, proofs, data, or exact runs.

### Steelman

What is the strongest honest formulation of the idea.

### Next Probe

What experiment, proof step, comparison, or reframing would most clarify it.

## Good Collaboration Questions

- What is actually working here?
- What explanation is tempting but still unproven?
- What is the fairest skeptical objection?
- What stronger control would answer it?
- If the broad claim fails, what narrower claim still stands?
- If the idea is real, what is the cleanest way to state its contribution?

## Anti-Patterns

- overclaiming because the idea feels fresh
- underclaiming because the codebase is unusual
- confusing explanation with evidence
- confusing a failed mechanism with a failed phenomenon
- burying historical signal instead of organizing it

## Success Condition

You are done with a tranche when:

- the strongest supported claim is clearer than before
- the weakest wording is gone or archived
- the best next experiment is easier to see
- the project feels more trustworthy without feeling less alive

## Short Version

Build new ideas the way a good research partner would:

- curious enough to notice signal
- skeptical enough to test it properly
- disciplined enough to label uncertainty
- generous enough to preserve the ambition at the center of the work
